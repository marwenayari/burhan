# Objections About the Sunnah and Hadith — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba

## BH-SUN-001 - Why do we need the Sunnah alongside the Quran?

### Question variants
- The Quran is enough for us
- What is the function of the Sunnah?

### How the objection is framed
The objection rests on the claim that the Quran alone is sufficient and the Sunnah is an unnecessary addition.

### Short answer
Abu Shahba explains that the Quran itself commands obedience to the Messenger and assigns him the function of explanation, and that the details of prayer, zakat, Hajj and other rites are known in practice through the Sunnah.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba explains that the Quran itself made obedience to the Messenger a principle and assigned him the function of explanation. The details of worship, such as the number of units of prayer, the amounts of zakat and the rites of Hajj, are not known from the Quran in detail, but are known in practice through the Sunnah. So the Sunnah is an explanation of the Quran, not a rival to it, and that some of its rulings stand independently does not mean it contradicts the Quran.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The Sunnah is an explanation of the Quran.
- Obeying the Messenger is a Quranic principle.
- That some rulings of the Sunnah stand independently does not mean they conflict with the Quran.

### Spoken answer
"The Quran itself commanded obedience to the Messenger and gave him the function of explanation. Abu Shahba points out that the details of prayer, zakat and Hajj are known only through the Sunnah, so it explains the Quran and does not replace it."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "The Status of the Sunnah in Religion", pp. 11–17

## BH-SUN-002 - Is the hadith of presenting the Sunnah to the Quran a proof for rejecting hadith?

### Question variants
- If we do not find the hadith in the Quran, we reject it
- Presenting the Sunnah to the Quran

### How the objection is framed
Those who deny the Sunnah cite a report that orders presenting hadiths to the Quran and rejecting what does not agree with it, in order to nullify the Sunnah's authority.

### Short answer
The author examines the report and judges it unestablished, and holds that a weak report cannot be used to cancel the foundation of the Sunnah's authority, which is established by the Quran.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba examines the report itself and judges it unestablished, so a weak report cannot be used to cancel a foundation established by the Quran, the authority of the Sunnah. The sound method is to examine whether the report is authentic first, then to reconcile the texts. Not every prophetic ruling is required to appear verbatim in the Quran in order to be accepted.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Examine whether the report is authentic first.
- Reconciling the texts.
- Not every prophetic ruling is required to appear verbatim in the Quran.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba examines the report on presenting the Sunnah to the Quran and finds it unestablished; a weak report cannot be used to cancel the Sunnah's authority, which the Quran itself established."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "The Hadith of Presenting the Sunnah to the Quran Is Fabricated", p. 18

## BH-SUN-003 - Does the prohibition on writing hadith prove it was not documented?

### Question variants
- The Prophet forbade writing hadith
- Why wasn't the Sunnah written down?

### How the objection is framed
The objection says the Prophet forbade writing hadith, which shows the Sunnah was not documented in his time.

### Short answer
The author reconciles the reports of prohibition and permission, and explains the early prohibition as a fear that hadith would be mixed with the Quran, while writing is established in the Prophetic era.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba reconciles the reports of prohibition with the reports of permission to write. He explains the early prohibition as a fear that hadith would be mixed with the Quran before the two were distinguished, while writing is established in the Prophetic era and early hadith sheets existed. The prohibition was not permanent, and context explains the difference between the reports.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The prohibition was not permanent.
- Early hadith sheets (suhuf) existed.
- Context explains the difference between the reports.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba reconciles the reports of prohibition and permission: the early ban was out of fear of mixing hadith with the Quran, and writing is established in the Prophetic era, so the ban was not permanent."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "The Prohibition on Writing Hadiths", pp. 20–22

## BH-SUN-004 - Was hadith not compiled until two centuries later?

### Question variants
- Al-Bukhari came 200 years later
- Late compilation weakens the Sunnah

### How the objection is framed
The objection claims that al-Bukhari and others compiled hadith about two centuries later, so it cannot be trusted.

### Short answer
The author distinguishes between early writing, general compilation (tadwin) and encyclopedic classification; the lateness of some major compilations does not mean the material was absent before them.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba distinguishes three stages: early individual writing, general compilation which was a stage of organization, and encyclopedic classification. The lateness of some major compilations does not mean the material was absent before them, since transmission was both oral and written. So the date of a book's classification is not the date the hadiths appeared.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Early individual writing.
- General compilation was a stage of organization.
- Transmission was both oral and written.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba separates early writing, general compilation and encyclopedic classification; the lateness of some major works does not mean the material did not exist and circulate before them."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "Writing Hadiths", "Compiling Them" and "Stages of Compilation", pp. 22–29

## BH-SUN-005 - Did hadith scholars care about the chain and neglect the text?

### Question variants
- Hadith criticism is formalistic
- Any text is accepted with a sound chain

### How the objection is framed
The objection describes the hadith scholars' criticism as formalistic, stopping at the chain and accepting any text as long as its chain is sound.

### Short answer
The author presents the criticism of both chains and texts together, and lists hidden defects ('ilal), irregularity (shudhudh), objectionability (nakara), inconsistency (idtirab) and contradiction among the tools of criticism.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba shows that the hadith scholars criticized both chains and texts, and made hidden defects, irregularity, objectionability, inconsistency and contradiction among their tools of criticism. Both chain and text are examined, and defects go beyond the names of narrators to the content of the report. Caution in criticism does not mean its absence.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Both chain and text are examined.
- Defects go beyond the names of narrators.
- Caution in criticism does not mean its absence.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba shows that the hadith scholars criticized both chains and texts, using tools such as hidden defects, irregularity, objectionability, inconsistency and contradiction, so the criticism was not merely formal."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "The Hadith Scholars' Care for Criticism and Understanding", pp. 28–35

## BH-SUN-006 - How did the hadith scholars authenticate narrators?

### Question variants
- Is it enough for a narrator to be righteous?
- A chain can easily be fabricated

### How the objection is framed
The objection doubts that a narrator's righteousness is enough to accept his report, and holds that chains can easily be fabricated.

### Short answer
The author presents the conditions of probity ('adala), precision (dabt) and continuity of the chain, as well as comparing reports and tracing errors, so personal righteousness alone is not sufficient.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba shows that personal righteousness alone is not enough; probity, precision and continuity of the chain are required, along with comparing reports and tracing errors. Probity and precision are two independent conditions, comparing routes exposes error, and the science of narrators and of defects is part of the method.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Probity and precision are two independent conditions.
- Comparing routes exposes error.
- The science of narrators and of defects is part of the method.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba presents the conditions of probity, precision and continuity, plus comparing reports and tracking errors, so a narrator's personal righteousness alone is not enough."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "Conditions of Acceptable Narration", pp. 30–33

## BH-SUN-007 - Did narration by meaning corrupt the Sunnah?

### Question variants
- The hadiths are not the Prophet's wording
- The narrators changed the texts

### How the objection is framed
The objection claims that what we have is not the Prophet's wording but the narrators' phrasing, and that they changed the texts.

### Short answer
The author affirms that narration by meaning was restricted by knowledge of the implications and by not changing the meaning, alongside wide care for narration by exact wording and comparison of routes.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba affirms that narration by meaning was not an absolute license, but was restricted by the narrator's knowledge of the implications of words and by not changing the meaning. It is not permitted when the wording changes the ruling. Alongside it there was wide care for narration by exact wording, and multiple routes reveal any consequential difference.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- It was not an absolute license.
- It is not permitted when the wording changes the ruling.
- Multiple routes reveal any consequential difference.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba affirms that narration by meaning was restricted by knowledge of the implications and by not changing the meaning, alongside wide care for exact wording and comparison of routes."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "Narration by Wording and by Meaning", pp. 35–38 and 73

## BH-SUN-008 - Did the scholars not care about the hadiths?

### Question variants
- They accepted hadith without criticism
- The hadith sciences are late

### How the objection is framed
The objection claims the scholars accepted hadiths without criticism and that the hadith sciences emerged late.

### Short answer
The author responds by presenting the journeys (rihla) and the works on narrators, defects and fabricated reports, and the distinguishing of grades of reports.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba responds by showing what the scholars actually did: journeys to gather and compare routes, works on narrators, defects and fabricated reports, and the distinguishing of grades of reports between sound and weak. The abundance of critical works is evidence that care for criticism existed.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The abundance of critical works.
- Travel to compare routes.
- Distinguishing sound from weak.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba responds by presenting the journeys to seek hadith, the works on narrators, defects and fabricated reports, and the grading of reports; the abundance of critical works shows the scholars' care."

## BH-SUN-009 - Were all the hadiths narrated by meaning?

### Question variants
- The whole Sunnah is the narrators' phrasing
- The Prophet's wording has not reached us

### How the objection is framed
The objection generalizes and says the whole Sunnah was narrated by meaning, so the Prophet's wording has not reached us.

### Short answer
The author rejects generalization and points to a group of narrators who took care with the wording, and to multiple routes that reveal the degree of agreement and difference.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba rejects this generalization. That some narration was by meaning does not prove it was general, and a group of narrators were strict about the wording. Multiple routes allow the comparison that reveals the degree of agreement and difference between the reports.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Some narration by meaning does not prove it was general.
- Some narrators were strict about the wording.
- Comparing routes is important.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba rejects the generalization; some narration by meaning does not prove it was universal, a group of narrators were strict about wording, and multiple routes reveal the degree of agreement and difference."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "His Claim That All Hadiths Were Narrated by Meaning", pp. 52–54

## BH-SUN-010 - Is the Quran's mass-transmission (tawatur) due to writing alone?

### Question variants
- The Quran is reliable because it was written, the Sunnah was not
- Writing is the cause of tawatur

### How the objection is framed
Skeptics compare the Quran and the Sunnah and say the Quran is reliable because it was written, whereas the Sunnah is not.

### Short answer
The author argues that the Quran was transmitted by memorization, writing and collective reception, that the Sunnah has different grades, and that scholars do not claim every hadith is on the level of the Quran.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba argues that the Quran was transmitted through memorization, writing and collective reception together, so writing is one element, not the only cause of its tawatur. The Sunnah includes mutawatir and single-narrator reports, with grades, and scholars do not claim that every hadith is on the level of the Quran. A difference in grade does not nullify authority altogether.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Writing is one element, not the only cause.
- The Sunnah includes mutawatir, ahad and grades.
- A difference in grade does not nullify authority altogether.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba explains that the Quran was transmitted by memorization, writing and collective reception, and that the Sunnah has grades; scholars do not claim every hadith equals the Quran, but a difference in grade does not cancel authority."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "His Claim That the Cause of the Quran's Tawatur Was Its Writing", pp. 54–55

## BH-SUN-011 - Did 'Umar imprison the Companions to prevent hadith?

### How the objection is framed
The objection accuses 'Umar ibn al-Khattab of imprisoning the Companions and preventing them from narrating hadith, taking this as evidence of the Companions' doubt about the Sunnah.

### Short answer
The author examines the reports and treats whatever is authentic of the strictness as caution against excessive narration and error, not as a denial of the Sunnah, and cites 'Umar's own use of it as evidence.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba examines the reports and the chains of the imprisonment reports. Whatever is authentic of 'Umar's strictness he treats as caution against excessive narration and error in transmission, not a denial of the Sunnah. The proof is 'Umar's own use of the Sunnah as evidence and his acting on it, which contradicts the claim that he rejected it.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Preventing excessive narration is not denying authority.
- Examining the chains of the imprisonment reports.
- 'Umar's acting on the Sunnah contradicts the claim that he rejected it.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba examines the reports and treats whatever is authentic of 'Umar's strictness as caution against excessive narration and error, not as denying the Sunnah, citing 'Umar's own reliance on it."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, "His Injustice to Our Master 'Umar", pp. 56–58

The author defends the hadith through its many routes and its narration from a large number of Companions, and considers it one of the best-known examples of tawatur among the hadith scholars.

It was used early in warning against fabrication. Whoever impugns it must explain its wide circulation.

### Evidence and line of reasoning

### Short answer
The author separates finding the content strange from the hadith ruling, and relies on the multiplicity of reports and the establishment of their foundations among the hadith scholars.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba separates finding the content strange from the hadith ruling on the report. A report about the unseen is not rejected merely for being strange, and the routes are collected before ruling. He relies on the multiplicity of reports and the establishment of their foundations among the hadith scholars, noting that not all the details are of the same grade.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- A report about the unseen is not rejected merely for being strange.
- Collect the routes before ruling.
- Not all the details are of the same grade.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba separates finding the content strange from the hadith ruling, and relies on the multiplicity of reports and the establishment of their foundations among the hadith scholars."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, pp. 98–100

## BH-SUN-025 - Is the large number of Abu Hurayra's hadiths evidence of fabrication?

### Question variants
- How did Abu Hurayra narrate thousands of hadiths?
- He converted late, so how did he narrate so much?

### How the objection is framed
The objector asks how Abu Hurayra narrated thousands of hadiths despite converting late, and sees in their number evidence of fabrication.

### Short answer
The author defends Abu Hurayra through his constant companionship, his devotion of his time, his long life and the many who took from him, noting that the numbers in the musnad collections include repetitions and routes.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba defends Abu Hurayra through his constant companionship with the Prophet, his devotion of his time to it, his long life and the many who took from him. He notes that the numbers in the musnad collections include repetitions and multiple routes, so the number of narrations is not the number of unique texts. Corroborating reports from other Companions also exist for what he narrated.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The number of narrations is not the number of unique texts.
- Devotion and companionship.
- Corroborating reports from other Companions exist.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba defends Abu Hurayra through his companionship, devotion, long life and the many who took from him, and notes that the numbers in the musnads include repetitions and routes."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, Chapter on Abu Hurayra, about p. 150 ff.

## BH-SUN-026 - Did Ka'b al-Ahbar feed Abu Hurayra hadiths?

### Question variants
- Abu Hurayra took from Ka'b
- Abu Hurayra's Israelite material

### How the objection is framed
The objection claims that Abu Hurayra took reports from Ka'b al-Ahbar and attributed them to the Prophet.

### Short answer
The author responds that merely hearing from Ka'b does not establish that what he heard from him was attributed to the Prophet, and that a mix-up in a specific narration would have to be proven.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba responds that merely hearing from Ka'b does not establish that what he heard from him was attributed to the Prophet, and a mix-up would have to be proven in a specific narration. One cannot generalize from a partial possibility, and the sources of narrations must be distinguished, since the marfu', the mawquf and the Israelite are different grades.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Distinguishing the sources of the narration.
- No generalizing from a partial possibility.
- The marfu', mawquf and Israelite are different grades.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba responds that Abu Hurayra's merely hearing from Ka'b does not establish that he attributed what he heard to the Prophet; a mix-up must be proven in a specific narration."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, about p. 157 ff.

## BH-SUN-027 - Are single-narrator reports (ahad) without authority?

### Question variants
- I accept only the mutawatir
- A hadith ahad is speculative so it is not acted upon

### How the objection is framed
Deniers say they accept only the mutawatir, and that a single-narrator hadith is speculative and not to be acted upon.

### Short answer
The author defends the authority of the sound single report in rulings, under its conditions, and rejects equating the lack of tawatur with the absence of trust.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba defends the authority of the sound single report in rulings, under its conditions. He rejects equating the lack of tawatur with the absence of trust: trust has degrees, and the conditions of criticism give the report probative value. The practice of the early generations with the single report is part of the argument for its authority.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Trust has degrees.
- The conditions of criticism give it probative value.
- The practice of the early generations (salaf) with the single report is part of the argument.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba defends the authority of the sound single report in rulings, under its conditions, and rejects equating the lack of tawatur with the absence of trust."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, Discussions of single and mutawatir narrations in the later section

## BH-SUN-028 - Does attacking al-Bukhari and Ibn Hajar bring down the Sunnah?

### Question variants
- Al-Bukhari is unreliable
- Ibn Hajar is inconsistent

### How the objection is framed
Some claim that attacking al-Bukhari or Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani brings down the Sunnah, or cite truncated quotations from their words.

### Short answer
The author responds by reviewing the quotations in their origins and contexts, and distinguishes between criticizing a particular ijtihad and discarding an imam or an entire method.

### Detailed answer
Abu Shahba responds by reviewing the quotations in their original sources and contexts, and by not cutting off the scholars' words. He distinguishes criticizing a particular ijtihad from discarding an imam or an entire method. A scholarly disagreement on one issue is not betrayal and does not overturn the whole method.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Return to the original text.
- Not cutting off the scholars' words.
- Scholarly disagreement is not betrayal.

### Spoken answer
"Abu Shahba responds by reviewing quotations in their original sources and contexts, distinguishing criticism of a particular ijtihad from discarding an imam or an entire method."

### Sources
- In Defense of the Sunnah and Refuting the Doubts of Orientalists and Contemporary Writers — Muhammad Muhammad Abu Shahba, Later section: the attack on al-Bukhari and Ibn Hajar
