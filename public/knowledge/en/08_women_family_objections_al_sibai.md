# Objections About Women and the Family — Mustafa al-Siba'i

## BH-WOM-001 - Did Islam consider women less human?

### Question variants
- Woman is half a human
- Man is better than woman

### How the objection is framed
The objection claims that Islam made woman half a human being and of less worth than man.

### Short answer
Al-Siba'i affirms that men and women share the essence of humanity, religious obligation, reward and punishment, and that differences in some rulings do not mean a deficiency in dignity.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i affirms that men and women share the essence of humanity, and share religious obligation, reward and punishment. A woman has an independent religious and moral capacity. The differences found in some rulings do not mean a deficiency in dignity or worth; a difference in ruling is not a disparity in humanity.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The unity of the human origin.
- Independent religious and moral capacity.
- Differences do not mean lesser worth.

### Spoken answer
"Al-Siba'i affirms that man and woman share the essence of humanity, obligation, reward and punishment, and that differences in some rulings do not mean a deficiency in dignity."

### Sources
- Al-Mar'a bayn al-Fiqh wa al-Qanun (Woman Between Islamic Jurisprudence and Law) — Mustafa al-Siba'i, "Islam's Principles on Women", about pp. 23–38

## BH-WOM-002 - Is a woman's testimony always half a man's?

### Question variants
- Two women for one man
- Women are less rational in court

### How the objection is framed
The objection says two women's testimony equals one man's in all cases, and that this shows a deficiency in women's reason.

### Short answer
The author explains that the verse on debt addresses the context of documenting a financial transaction and is not an absolute rule for all testimony, and that there are areas in which a woman's testimony alone is accepted.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i explains that the verse on debt addresses the context of documenting a financial transaction, so it is not an absolute rule for all testimony, and there are areas in which a woman's testimony alone is accepted. The financial context matters, the categories of proof differ according to the event, and testimony is not a measure of a person's worth.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The financial context matters.
- Testimony is not the measure of a person's worth.
- The categories of proof differ according to the event.

### Spoken answer
"Al-Siba'i explains that the verse on debt addresses the context of documenting a financial transaction, not an absolute rule for all testimony, and that there are areas where a woman's testimony alone is accepted."

- Al-Mar'a bayn al-Fiqh wa al-Qanun (Woman Between Islamic Jurisprudence and Law) — Mustafa al-Siba'i, "Some Differences — Testimony", pp. 27–28

## BH-WOM-003 - Is a woman's inheritance always half a man's?

### Question variants
- For the male, the share of two females
- Inheritance wrongs women

### How the objection is framed
The objection says "for the male, the share of two females" means a woman always receives half of a man, and that this wrongs her.

### Short answer
The author shows that inheritance shares are varied, that in some cases a woman equals or exceeds a man, and he links the half-share cases to differing financial obligations.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i shows that inheritance shares are varied and that the half rule is not general. Kinship and the composition of the heirs change the shares, and in some cases a woman equals or exceeds a man. The half-share cases are tied to differing financial obligations, such as maintenance and financial responsibility, which are part of the rationale.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The half rule is not general.
- Kinship and the composition of the heirs change the shares.
- Maintenance and financial responsibility are part of the rationale.

### Spoken answer
"Al-Siba'i shows that inheritance shares are varied, that in some cases a woman equals or exceeds a man, and he links the half-share cases to differing financial obligations."

### Sources
- Al-Mar'a bayn al-Fiqh wa al-Qanun (Woman Between Islamic Jurisprudence and Law) — Mustafa al-Siba'i, "On Inheritance", about pp. 29–32

## BH-WOM-004 - Does a woman's blood money (diya) mean her life is worth less?

### Question variants
- A woman's diya is half a man's
- A woman's life is worth less

### How the objection is framed
The objection says a woman's diya is half a man's, so her life is worth less.

### Short answer
The author separates the sanctity of life and retribution (qisas) from financial compensation, and presents the jurisprudential disagreement over diya within the structure of financial obligations, not as treating women as less human.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i separates the sanctity of life and retribution on one side from financial compensation on the other. Diya is not the price of a human being, and qisas relates to the sanctity of life. He presents the jurisprudential disagreement over diya within the structure of financial obligations, so it is a financial juristic matter, not a judgment on dignity or on women's humanity.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Diya is not the price of a human being.
- Qisas relates to the sanctity of life.
- It is a financial juristic matter, not a judgment on dignity.

### Spoken answer
"Al-Siba'i separates the sanctity of life and retribution from financial compensation, and presents the disagreement over diya within the structure of financial obligations, not as treating women as less human."

### Sources
- Al-Mar'a bayn al-Fiqh wa al-Qanun (Woman Between Islamic Jurisprudence and Law) — Mustafa al-Siba'i, "A Woman's Diya", about pp. 32–33

The objection says Islam bars women from heading the state, which means barring them from all public work.

### Short answer
Al-Siba'i presents the juristic opinion on the supreme leadership and distinguishes it from other positions and public participation, so he does not make the restriction a judgment on women's human capacity as a whole.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i presents the juristic opinion on the supreme leadership and distinguishes it from other positions and public participation. The texts discuss one specific political office, and the supreme leadership is not every public position. He does not make the restriction on that office a judgment on women's human capacity as a whole, since dignity is not tied to office.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The supreme leadership is not every public position.
- The texts discuss one specific political office.
- Dignity is not tied to office.

### Spoken answer

- The judiciary intervenes in cases of abuse.

## BH-WOM-011 - Why did Islam permit polygyny?

### Question variants
- Why four for a man?
- Polygyny is unjust

### How the objection is framed
The objection asks why Islam allowed a man more than one wife, and sees this as wronging women.

### Short answer
Al-Siba'i defends polygyny as a restricted permission, not an obligation, tying it to social and personal circumstances and requiring justice and capacity.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i defends polygyny as a restricted permission, not an obligation: it is permitted, not commanded. He ties it to social and personal circumstances and requires justice, maintenance and capacity. He also discusses the realistic alternatives when it is banned.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Permitted, not obligatory.
- Justice, maintenance and capacity are conditions.
- He discusses the realistic alternatives where it is banned.

### Spoken answer
"Al-Siba'i defends polygyny as a restricted permission, not an obligation, tying it to social and personal circumstances and requiring justice and capacity."

The objection cites "two, three or four" to claim polygyny is permitted without restriction, or that justice in it is impossible.

The author stresses that the permission is tied to justice in the outward rights and to capacity, and that the fear of not being just directs one to a single wife.

Al-Siba'i stresses that the permission is tied to justice in the outward rights and to capacity. Justice is a condition that is part of the ruling, meaning justice in what a person controls: maintenance, nights and treatment. Mere inclination of the heart is outside full capacity. Whoever fears he will not be just is directed to a single wife.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The condition of justice is part of the ruling.
- Justice in what a person controls: maintenance, nights and treatment.
- Mere inclination of the heart is outside full capacity.

## BH-WOM-015 - Why is divorce originally in the man's hands?

### Question variants
- The man divorces and the woman doesn't
- This is discrimination

### How the objection is framed
The objection says divorce is in the man's hands alone and a woman cannot end the marriage, which is discrimination.

### Short answer
Al-Siba'i presents the jurists' rationale based on the structure of financial obligations, but shows that the woman has khul' (divorce by redemption) and judicial separation, and can stipulate delegation of divorce.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i presents the jurists' rationale based on the structure of financial obligations, since the financial consequences fall on the husband. But he shows that the woman has several ways to end the marriage, including khul' and judicial separation, and can stipulate delegation of divorce. The judiciary addresses harm.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- The financial consequences fall on the husband.
- The woman has several ways to end the marriage.
- The judiciary addresses harm.

### Spoken answer
"Al-Siba'i presents the jurists' rationale based on financial obligations, but shows that the woman has khul' and judicial separation and can stipulate delegation of divorce."

## BH-WOM-025 - Does women's liberation mean imitating the West?

### Question variants
- Rejecting the Western model is rejecting women's rights
- Women's rights are a Western idea

### How the objection is framed
The objection says rejecting the Western model on women's affairs is rejecting their rights, and that women's rights are a Western idea.

### Short answer
Al-Siba'i distinguishes between defending women's rights and adopting every Western model of family, sexuality and work, and sees reform as possible from within the Islamic frame of reference.

### Detailed answer
Al-Siba'i distinguishes defending women's rights from adopting every Western model of family, sexuality and work. Reform is not imitation, and it can be carried out from within the Islamic frame of reference. He criticizes Eastern customs as well, and rights do not require a single civilizational model.

### Evidence and line of reasoning
- Reform is not imitation.
- He also criticizes Eastern customs.
- Rights do not require a single civilizational model.

"Al-Siba'i distinguishes defending women's rights from adopting every Western model of family, sexuality and work, and sees reform as possible from within the Islamic frame of reference."
